\section{Simulation and Experimental Design}
\label{sec:design}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{Geometric Brownian Motion}

Under GBM with zero drift,
\[
  S_{k+1}=S_k
  \exp\!\left(-\frac{1}{2}\sigma^2\Delta t
  +\sigma\sqrt{\Delta t}\,Z_{k+1}\right),
  \qquad Z_{k+1}\sim N(0,1),
\]
so the grid-point simulation is exact.

\subsection{Heston full-truncation discretization}

The continuous-time dynamics are
\begin{align}
  \dd S_t&=S_t\sqrt{v_t}\,\dd W_t^S,\\
  \dd v_t&=\kappa_v(\theta_v-v_t)\,\dd t
  +\xi\sqrt{v_t}\,\dd W_t^v,
  \qquad \dd\langle W^S,W^v\rangle_t=\rho\,\dd t.
\end{align}
Let $\widetilde v_k$ be the Euler auxiliary variable and
$v_k^+=\max(\widetilde v_k,0)$. A fully specified full-truncation/log-Euler
step is
\begin{align}
  \widetilde v_{k+1}
  &=\widetilde v_k
  +\kappa_v(\theta_v-v_k^+)\Delta t
  +\xi\sqrt{v_k^+\Delta t}\,Z_{k+1}^{v},
  \label{eq:heston_v}\\
  \log S_{k+1}
  &=\log S_k-\frac{1}{2}v_k^+\Delta t
  +\sqrt{v_k^+\Delta t}\,Z_{k+1}^{S},
  \label{eq:heston_s}
\end{align}
where
\[
  Z_{k+1}^{S}=\rho Z_{k+1}^{v}
  +\sqrt{1-\rho^2}\,Z_{k+1}^{\perp}
\]
and $Z^v,Z^{\perp}$ are independent standard normals. The auxiliary variable
$\widetilde v_{k+1}$ may be negative; non-negativity applies to the truncated
variance $v_{k+1}^+$ used in the spot diffusion and as a network feature. This
is the relevant property of full truncation, not literal non-negativity of the
untruncated Euler state \citep{lord2010}.

The Heston parameters used here violate the Feller condition:
\[
  2\kappa_v\theta_v=0.16<\xi^2=0.25.
\]
Consequently, discretization checks with finer substeps or a higher-accuracy
scheme are particularly important before treating numerical differences as
model effects.

\subsection{Parameters}

The common parameters are
\[
  S_0=K=100,\qquad T=30/252,\qquad n=30,
  \qquad \alpha=0.95.
\]
For GBM, $\sigma=0.20$. For Heston,
\[
  v_0=\theta_v=0.04,\qquad \kappa_v=2.0,
  \qquad \xi=0.5,\qquad \rho=-0.7.
\]
The archived test results use 30,000 simulated paths. A confirmatory
comparison would evaluate all strategies on the same held-out paths and estimate
uncertainty in their differences by paired resampling.

\subsection{Benchmarks and their interpretation}

The main benchmark is the Black--Scholes delta
\[
  \Delta_{\mathrm{BS}}(S_k,\tau_k)
  =\Phi\!\left(
    \frac{\log(S_k/K)+\tfrac12\sigma^2\tau_k}
         {\sigma\sqrt{\tau_k}}
  \right),
\]
with zero interest rate. It is rebalanced on the same grid and charged the same
cost function as the neural policy.

This benchmark is standard but limited. Under discrete rebalancing, it is not
known to minimize terminal CVaR, even when transaction costs are zero. Under
positive costs, it is deliberately cost-blind. Therefore, a lower point estimate relative to this rule concerns only
that particular comparison and does not imply global or near-global optimality. Relevant cost-aware benchmarks include a shrunk delta
$\lambda\Delta_{\mathrm{BS}}$, a no-trade band, less-frequent rebalancing, and
an inventory-aware policy.

In the Heston experiment, the network observes $v_k^+$, whereas the benchmark
uses a fixed volatility of 20 percent. The comparison is therefore asymmetric
in both information and model flexibility. It is retained only as a
misspecified-benchmark exercise. A like-for-like Heston comparison requires at
least a Black--Scholes delta with $\sigma_k=\sqrt{v_k^+}$, an optimized fixed
volatility, a Heston delta, and network ablations with and without the variance
feature.

\subsection{Statistical interpretation}

The experiment set reports one training realization per displayed policy and
one finite test sample per comparison. No confidence intervals across training
seeds are available. Accordingly:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item differences are point estimates, not significance statements;
  \item monotonicity in the displayed cost sweep refers to the five observed
  point estimates only;
  \item the difference between independently trained policies at the same
  hyperparameters cannot be labelled ``within expected run-to-run variance''
  without repeated runs;
  \item confirmatory evidence requires multiple training seeds and paired
  bootstrap intervals on a held-out test sample.
\end{itemize}
